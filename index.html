About

Why ConnectedSE

Connected software rarely lives in one place. One user-visible function can span a mobile app, a vendor cloud service, a device firmware image, and a communication protocol, each developed and updated independently and often partly opaque to the engineers who depend on it.

This makes correctness, security, and privacy hard to assess across component boundaries. Large language models (LLMs) offer new support for program understanding, test generation, vulnerability analysis, debugging, and maintenance, but applying them here requires methods that link heterogeneous artifacts and validate their conclusions against observable behavior.

ConnectedSE brings together the mobile, embedded, protocol, cloud, and program-analysis communities around LLM-assisted engineering of connected and cyber-physical software ecosystems. It covers both the use of LLMs to understand and improve connected software and the engineering of connected systems that incorporate LLM-based components.

Our guiding question. What evidence is needed to make reliable claims about the behavior of a connected system?
Call for Papers

Five themes

Different claims about a connected system require different kinds of evidence. The workshop is organized around five themes, each tied to one evidence problem.

1

Cross-artifact understanding and traceability

Techniques that connect GUI actions, app code, APIs, cloud services, firmware, protocols, and execution traces, recover dependencies and data flows, or interpret undocumented interfaces.

Evidence problem: linkage
2

End-to-end testing, debugging, and evolution

End-to-end test generation, state exploration, oracle construction, fault localization, and repair validation across app, cloud, and device updates.

Evidence problem: oracles
3

Regulatory evidence and opaque components

Checking privacy and security obligations across app code, network traffic, backends, and devices, including vendor clouds, closed firmware, and over-the-air updates.

Evidence problem: defensibility
4

Assurance of LLM-enabled connected systems

Validating generated automation rules and API interactions, keeping actions within authorization boundaries, linking model decisions to observable effects, and handling failures safely.

Evidence problem: attribution
5

Benchmarks, evaluation, and reproducibility

Benchmarks, datasets, testbeds, replications, negative results, and comparisons with non-LLM baselines across mobile apps, cloud services, and devices.

Evidence problem: comparability
★

A shared deliverable

The five themes converge on a specification for reproducible benchmarks, naming representative tasks, required artifacts, suitable oracles, and reproducibility assumptions. We will draft it together at the workshop.

Scope

Companion-app ecosystems, including smart homes and wearables, are our reference setting. We also welcome work on mobile software more broadly, and on medical and health devices, industrial IoT and operational technology, smart buildings and energy systems, consumer electronics and firmware, voice assistants and edge-AI products, robotics, connected-vehicle telematics, and over-the-air update infrastructure.

One boundary is enough. Work reasoning across app and cloud, cloud and device, app and device, or app and external services does not need to cover the whole chain. Single-artifact studies are in scope when they make their relationship to ecosystem behavior explicit or provide a substantive advance for LLM-assisted mobile software engineering.

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to

  • LLM-assisted program comprehension across apps, clouds, and devices
  • Recovery of cross-component dependencies and data flows
  • Interpretation of undocumented APIs and protocols
  • LLM-guided GUI, API, and protocol testing and fuzzing
  • Oracles for remote and physical state
  • Fault localization and repair across app, cloud, and firmware updates
  • Privacy and security compliance checking (e.g., GDPR, Cyber Resilience Act)
  • Analysis of opaque components: vendor clouds, closed firmware, OTA updates
  • Assurance and authorization of LLM agents acting on connected systems
  • Validation of generated automation rules
  • Benchmarks, datasets, testbeds, and digital twins
  • Replications, negative results, and comparisons with non-LLM baselines
  • Industrial experience reports

Submission

Contribution types

Archival track

Published in the ACM Digital Library as extended abstracts, free of article processing charges.

  • Research, experience, replication, negative-result, tool, and dataset or benchmark extended abstracts: up to 5 pages including references.
  • Vision, emerging-ideas, and position extended abstracts: up to 3 pages including references.

Submissions undergo double-anonymous review by at least three program committee members. Borderline submissions may be offered a poster or lightning-talk slot.

Non-archival track

Presented at the workshop but not published, so the work stays free to be submitted elsewhere.

  • Encore talks: a one-page abstract referring to work published or accepted elsewhere in the previous 24 months.
  • Late-breaking results, lightning talks, and posters: 1 to 2 pages.

Work under review elsewhere is eligible for this track only.

Guidelines

  • Submissions must follow the ACM format adopted by FSE 2027. Formatting details and the submission link will be published here.
  • Reviewing criteria are relevance, technical credibility, clarity, evidence appropriate to the contribution type, and ability to spark discussion. Vision, position, and emerging-ideas papers do not need a mature evaluation, and preliminary results are welcome.
  • For an archival contribution to appear in the proceedings, at least one of its authors must register for the workshop.
  • FSE 2027 is an in-person event, and each accepted contribution must be presented on site.
  • Following the SIGSOFT policy, the organizers and their directly supervised students, postdocs, and employees do not submit to ConnectedSE.
  • As a published ACM author, you and your co-authors are subject to all ACM Publications Policies, including ACM's new Publications Policy on Research Involving Human Participants and Subjects.
